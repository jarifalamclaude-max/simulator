BRACU DUBURI ROV simulator · ArduSub 4.5.7

Fly the robot before it gets wet.

DUBURI is a virtual pool that runs in the browser. Give it your ROV's 3D design and your Pixhawk parameter file, and it flies the vehicle with a line-by-line port of the ArduSub 4.5.7 controllers on top of a Fossen 6-DOF hydrodynamic model.

Guide

Quick start#

Nine steps from your CAD file to a tuned .param file. Click any screenshot to enlarge it.

  1. Open the simulator

    Open jarifalam13.github.io/DUBURI_SIM in Chrome or Edge. The first screen offers Fly the sample first (an 8-thruster ROV already loaded with the DUBURI Pixhawk parameters) or Upload my design.

  2. Upload your design

    Click Upload design in the top bar, or drop the file anywhere. In Vehicle, set the file units (Fusion 360 and SolidWorks export in mm), which axis points forward and which points up. Check that the red X arrow in the 3D view points to the bow.

    If the parts are named (T200_1, Thruster 2…) or the thrusters have ducts, they are found automatically and a custom frame is built from them. Click Accept, Flip a thruster that pushes the wrong way, or Discard.

  3. Enter the real mass and centre of gravity

    Scroll down in Vehicle to Mass and centre of gravity. Enter the mass in air from a scale and the CG from your CAD mass properties. The In the water box then shows whether the vehicle floats or sinks, the distance between centre of buoyancy and CG (GM), and the natural roll and pitch periods. Make neutral sets the mass for neutral buoyancy.

  4. Set the pool and water

    Pool sets the pool length, width and depth (default 25 × 12 × 4 m), a flat or sloped floor, fresh or sea water, current, turbulence and waves, the depth sensor (Bar02, Bar30 or custom) and the depth rating of your housings. Tick Positioning system present only if the real vehicle has a DVL or USBL: Position Hold, Auto and Guided need it.

  5. Check the frame

    Frame shows every ArduSub FRAME_CONFIG as a card, plus Automatic, which builds the mixer from where your thrusters really are. Under Try it, move a slider: the thrusters that fire light up (orange forward, blue reverse) and the resulting force or torque is printed above the sliders.

  6. Arm and fly

    Press Enter to arm. W/S forward and back, A/D yaw, Q/E strafe, R/F up and down. Keys 1–6 change mode. A gamepad works too (see Flying).

  7. Hold depth and watch the graphs

    Press 3 for Depth Hold and G for the live graphs. Dashed lines are what ArduSub asks for, solid lines what the vehicle did. Export CSV saves the data.

  8. Tune

    Tuning lists the ArduSub gains and runs a step test on a fresh copy of the vehicle: rise time, overshoot, settling time and steady error are measured. AUTO-TUNE (top bar) computes added mass and damping for your design and suggests gains; nothing changes until you click Apply.

  9. Take it to the vehicle

    In Params, Save (Mission Planner) or Save (QGC) writes every parameter of your file plus the ones you changed. Load it on the Pixhawk with QGroundControl or Mission Planner and check the changed values before the first dive.

Thruster auto-detection#

Runs after every upload, and from Find thrusters in my design in Vehicle or Frame.

FormatPart names keptHow thrusters are found
OBJ (recommended)yes objects and groupsBy name, then by shape
ASCII STL, multi-bodyyes one solid per bodyBy name, then by shape
Binary STLnoBy shape only
PLYnoBy shape only
  1. Names. Parts called T200_1, Thruster 2, Assembly/T200 (4)/Nozzle are recognised; the number becomes the motor number. Mounts, brackets and screws are skipped.
  2. Shape. The mesh is split into connected parts; each is tested as a surface of revolution. A duct needs a complete outer and inner wall. Outside diameter ≈ 100 mm → T200, ≈ 140 mm → T500, otherwise a generic thruster sized from the inside diameter. Pipes, rods and housings are rejected.
  3. Direction and order. Geometry gives the axis but not which way it pushes, so each thruster's force and torque is matched against the rows of your FRAME_CONFIG table (with an optimal one-to-one assignment). That picks the push direction and the motor number. Spin direction follows MOT_n_DIRECTION.
From Fusion 360 (.f3z)

The browser cannot read Fusion archives. Open the design in Fusion, rename the thruster components T200_1 … T200_8 to match your motor outputs, then File → Export → OBJ (or Utilities → 3D Print → OBJ). Upload with units = mm.

Typical designs take 50–90 ms; a 370 000-triangle mesh about 0.45 s. A short hollow housing 85–115 mm across can look like a T200: check the list and discard it.

How it works

Architecture#

Two parts: a set-up pipeline that turns your design into a hydrodynamic model once, and a closed loop that runs 400 times per simulated second. Click a box for details, or trace one loop.

Firmware tick 2.5 ms · physics step 1.25 ms (2 per tick)
How it works

The Fossen 6-DOF model#

The equations of motion used in marine robotics, from Thor I. Fossen, Handbook of Marine Craft Hydrodynamics and Motion Control (Wiley, 2011/2021). Implemented in src/physics/body.js.

A rigid body in water can move in six ways at once: three translations and three rotations. Those are the six degrees of freedom (DOF). Fossen's model is Newton's second law written for all six together, with the extra forces water adds: the water the vehicle carries along when it accelerates, the drag, and buoyancy.

DOFMotionVelocityForce / momentPosition / angle
1Surge (forward)\(u\)\(X\)\(x\) north
2Sway (right)\(v\)\(Y\)\(y\) east
3Heave (down)\(w\)\(Z\)\(z\) depth
4Roll\(p\)\(K\)\(\phi\)
5Pitch\(q\)\(M\)\(\theta\)
6Yaw\(r\)\(N\)\(\psi\)

Frames: world North-East-Down with the origin at the still-water surface in the middle of the pool; body Forward-Right-Down with the origin at the centre of gravity (CG). Positive roll is right side down, positive pitch nose up, positive yaw nose right (ArduPilot convention).

\[ \underbrace{M\,\dot{\nu}}_{\text{inertia}} + \underbrace{C(\nu_r)\,\nu_r}_{\text{Coriolis, Munk}} + \underbrace{D(\nu_r)\,\nu_r}_{\text{drag}} + \underbrace{g(\eta)}_{\text{weight, buoyancy}} = \underbrace{\tau}_{\text{thrusters, contact, waves}} \qquad \dot{\eta} = J(\eta)\,\nu \]
\(\eta\)position and attitude in the world frame (here a position and a unit quaternion)
\(\nu\)body velocities \([u,v,w,p,q,r]\); \(\nu_r\) is the velocity relative to the water
\(M\)6×6 inertia: the vehicle's own mass and inertia plus the added mass of water it pushes around
\(C\)forces that appear when the vehicle moves and turns at the same time, including the Munk moment that turns a moving body broadside
\(D\)water resistance, a linear part (slow speeds) and a quadratic part (\(\propto\) speed²)
\(g\)weight at the CG and buoyancy at the centre of buoyancy (CB): their offset is the righting moment

The code solves one linear system per evaluation. Everything except the inertia term is moved to the right-hand side:

\[ \left(M_{RB}+M_F+M_{A,e}\right)\dot{\nu} = \tau_{\text{grav}}+\tau_B+\tau_{FK}+\tau_D - C_{RB}(\nu)\nu - C_F(\nu)\nu - C_A(\nu_r)\nu_r + \tau_{\text{thr}}+\tau_c \]
\(M_F,\ C_F\)water that has flooded a leaking housing, a point mass at the CB
\(\tau_{FK}\)Froude–Krylov force from accelerating water (waves, gusts)
\(\tau_c\)contact with the pool floor and walls
body.js:1-4, 223-442 · the 6×6 matrix is inverted by Gauss–Jordan with partial pivoting (body.js:25-43)

State vector, 13 numbers: \(y=[x,y,z,\ q_w,q_x,q_y,q_z,\ u,v,w,\ p,q,r]\).

Kinematics: from body velocity to motion in the pool

Position changes with the body velocity rotated into the world frame. Attitude is a unit quaternion (no gimbal lock), turned into roll, pitch and yaw only for display, with ArduPilot's 3-2-1 order.

\[ \dot{p}^n = R(q)\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\w\end{bmatrix},\qquad \dot{q}=\tfrac12\,q\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \omega\end{bmatrix},\qquad \omega=[p,q,r]^\top \]
\[ R(q)=\begin{bmatrix}1-2(q_y^2+q_z^2) & 2(q_xq_y-q_wq_z) & 2(q_xq_z+q_wq_y)\\ 2(q_xq_y+q_wq_z) & 1-2(q_x^2+q_z^2) & 2(q_yq_z-q_wq_x)\\ 2(q_xq_z-q_wq_y) & 2(q_yq_z+q_wq_x) & 1-2(q_x^2+q_y^2)\end{bmatrix} \]
core/math.js:140-148, body.js:444-450

Water velocity (current, turbulence, wave orbits) is rotated into the body frame, and only the linear part is made relative; the water is not rotating.

\[ v_c^b = R^\top v_f^n(x,y,z),\qquad \nu_r=[\,u-u_c,\ v-v_c,\ w-w_c,\ p,\ q,\ r\,]^\top \]
body.js:232-236

Mass and added mass

Because the body origin is the CG, the rigid-body inertia is block-diagonal. When the vehicle accelerates it also has to accelerate some water: that is the added mass \(M_A\), a full 6×6 matrix computed by the panel method (see Added mass and damping). It shrinks with the submerged fraction \(\sigma\) at the surface and grows near the floor, walls and surface (image effect).

\[ M_{RB}=\begin{bmatrix} m I_3 & 0\\ 0 & I_g\end{bmatrix},\qquad M_{A,e}=\sigma\,S_f\,M_A\,S_f,\qquad S_f=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\sqrt{f_1},\sqrt{f_2},\sqrt{f_3},1,1,1\right) \]
\[ f_i \leftarrow f_i\left(1+\begin{cases}\tfrac38\,r^3 & i=\text{axis normal to the nearby plane}\\[2pt] \tfrac3{16}\,r^3 & \text{other axes}\end{cases}\right),\qquad r=\frac{a_{eq}}{\max(h,a_{eq})},\quad a_{eq}=\sqrt[3]{\tfrac{3V}{4\pi}} \]
\[ M_F=m_f\begin{bmatrix} I_3 & -S(r_b)\\ S(r_b) & -S(r_b)^2\end{bmatrix},\qquad m_f=\rho\,V_{\text{flood}} \]
\(h\)distance from the CG to the floor, the surface (treated as a rigid lid) or a wall
\(S(a)\)skew matrix, \(S(a)b=a\times b\); \(r_b\) is the CB relative to the CG
body.js:64-67, 95-138 · M is rebuilt only when σ, an image factor or the flooded mass changes by more than 0.2 %

Coriolis, centripetal and Munk terms

A body that moves and rotates at once feels forces that do no work but redirect momentum. For the water this is the Munk moment: an elongated hull moving at an angle is turned broadside, which is why ROVs need yaw control at speed.

\[ C_{RB}(\nu)\nu=\begin{bmatrix} m\,\omega\times v\\ \omega\times(I_g\,\omega)\end{bmatrix},\qquad C_A(\nu_r)\nu_r=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\times p_t\\ v_r\times p_t+\omega\times p_r\end{bmatrix} \]
\[ p_t=A_{11}v_r+A_{12}\,\omega,\qquad p_r=A_{21}v_r+A_{22}\,\omega \qquad \left(M_{A,e}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\A_{21}&A_{22}\end{bmatrix}\right) \]
body.js:327-363 · this is Fossen's \(C_A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-S(p_t)\\-S(p_t)&-S(p_r)\end{bmatrix}\)

Damping

Fully coupled 6×6 linear and quadratic matrices. The linear part dominates at low speed, the quadratic part (form drag, \(\tfrac12\rho C_d A v|v|\)) at higher speed. Damping fades as the vehicle leaves the water, and grows near the surface where it makes waves.

\[ \tau_{D,i}=-s_d\,\beta_i\sum_{j=1}^{6}\Big(D_{L,ij}\,\nu_{r,j}+D_{Q,ij}\,|\nu_{r,j}|\,\nu_{r,j}\Big),\qquad s_d=\max(\sigma,0.02),\quad \beta_i=\begin{cases}1+0.6\cdot 4\sigma(1-\sigma) & i\le 3\\ 1 & i>3\end{cases} \]
body.js:312-325 · surface wave-making factor 0.6 (environment.js:32)

Buoyancy and gravity

Weight acts at the CG, so it never makes a moment. Buoyancy acts at the CB; when the CB is above the CG the pair makes the righting moment that keeps the vehicle level. Water gets denser with depth and the hull can compress, so buoyancy changes slightly with depth. At the surface the hull is cut into volume elements, each partly submerged under the local wave height.

\[ f_G^b = m g\,R^\top e_3,\qquad b_s=\frac{\rho(p_g)}{\rho_0}\,\max\!\left(0.5,\ 1-\beta p_g\right) \]
\[ \text{submerged: } f_B^b=-\rho_0 g V b_s\,R^\top e_3,\quad m_B=r_b\times f_B^b \qquad \text{at the surface: } \phi_e=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{d_e+h_{z,e}}{2h_{z,e}},0,1\right),\ \ \sigma=\frac{\sum_e v_e\phi_e}{V} \]
\(p_g\)gauge pressure at the CG depth; \(\beta\) hull compressibility [1/Pa]
\(d_e,\ h_{z,e}\)depth of element \(e\) below the local wave surface, and its vertical half-height
body.js:243-288 · natural roll period shown in Vehicle: \(T_{roll}=2\pi\sqrt{(I_{xx}+A_{44})/(\rho g V\,GM)}\)

A housing past its depth rating leaks through an orifice against the air inside it, compressed by Boyle's law; the flooded water adds weight and mass and wets the leak probe:

\[ p_{in}=\frac{p_0V_a}{V_a-V_f},\qquad \dot V_f=0.62\,A_{\text{leak}}\sqrt{\frac{2\,(p_{out}-p_{in})}{\rho_0}} \]
body.js:195-213

Water, pressure, waves and current

Density comes from temperature and salinity (Tanaka 2001 for fresh water plus the UNESCO EOS-80 salt term), viscosity from Vogel's equation. Pressure is integrated down the water column with a compressible equation of state, so a Bar30 reads what it would in a real pool.

\[ \frac{dp_g}{dz}=g\,\rho(p_g),\qquad \rho(p_g)=\frac{\rho_0}{1-\bar p/K(S,T,\bar p)},\qquad p_{abs}=p_{atm}+p_g \]
environment.js:141-174, water.js:19-43 · K is the EOS-80 secant bulk modulus

Waves are linear (Airy) waves over finite depth. The wavenumber solves the dispersion relation by Newton's method; particle velocity and acceleration decay with depth.

\[ \omega^2=g\,k\tanh(kh),\qquad \eta=A\cos\varphi,\qquad \varphi=k(x\cos\beta+y\sin\beta)-\omega t \]
\[ u=A\omega\frac{\cosh k(h-z)}{\sinh kh}\cos\varphi,\qquad w=-A\omega\frac{\sinh k(h-z)}{\sinh kh}\sin\varphi \]
\(A,\ T_w,\ \beta\)wave amplitude, period \(2\pi/\omega\), heading (Pool & water); off by default
environment.js:101-236

Accelerating water pushes the vehicle even when it is still: the Froude–Krylov force plus the added-mass reaction. Without it a neutral vehicle would not follow the waves.

\[ \tau_{FK}=M_{A,e}[:,1{:}3]\;\dot{\nu}_c+\begin{bmatrix}F_{FK}\\ r_b\times F_{FK}\end{bmatrix},\qquad F_{FK}=\rho_0 V b_s\,a_f^b,\qquad \dot{\nu}_c=a_f^b-\omega\times v_c^b \]
body.js:294-309 · near the surface only the horizontal part is applied

Turbulence is a first-order Gauss–Markov gust, then smoothed so it can be differentiated:

\[ x_{k+1}=a\,x_k+\sigma_t\sqrt{1-a^2}\;\xi_k,\qquad a=e^{-\Delta t/\tau},\quad \xi\sim\mathcal N(0,1) \]
core/rng.js:40-56, environment.js:177-188 · vertical intensity is half the horizontal

Floor and walls

Contact points are the extreme corners of the voxelised hull in 26 directions. Each one is tested against the floor (flat or sloped) and the four walls separately. A spring–damper pushes it out with Coulomb friction; the gains are capped from the vehicle's mass and the step size so the integration stays stable for a 2 kg or a 30 kg vehicle.

\[ F_n=k\,\delta-c\,(V\cdot n)\quad(\delta>0,\ F_n>0),\qquad F_t=-\mu F_n\min\!\left(1,\frac{|v_t|}{v_{reg}}\right)\frac{v_t}{|v_t|} \]
\[ k=\min\!\left(k_{user},\ m_{pt}\,\omega_c^2\right),\quad \omega_c=\frac{0.5}{\Delta t},\qquad c\le\max\!\left(1.4\sqrt{k\,m_{pt}},\ 0.9\,\frac{m_{pt}}{\Delta t}\right) \]
body.js:140-163, 390-433 · defaults k = 30 000 N/m, c = 900 N·s/m, μ = 0.5

Integration

Each 1.25 ms physics step: thrusters, battery and flooding are advanced once; then classic 4th-order Runge–Kutta integrates the state, split into more sub-steps when damping would make it stiff; the quaternion is renormalised after each.

\[ y_{k+1}=y_k+\tfrac{h}{6}\left(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4\right),\qquad q\leftarrow\frac{q}{\lVert q\rVert} \]
\[ n=\min\!\left(64,\ \max\!\left(1,\ \left\lceil\tfrac{\lambda\,\Delta t}{2}\right\rceil\right)\right),\qquad \lambda=\max_i\frac{s_d\left(|D_{L,ii}|+2|D_{Q,ii}\,\nu_i|\right)}{M_{ii}},\qquad h=\frac{\Delta t}{n} \]
body.js:465-504 · physics 800 Hz, firmware 400 Hz → 2 physics steps per firmware tick (simulation.js:80-84) · with damping off, energy is conserved to better than 10⁻⁴
Where the code departs from textbook Fossen
  • Added mass changes with submergence and wall distance but is frozen within a step; the \(\dot M_A\nu\) term is not modelled.
  • Quadratic damping uses the column form \(D_{Q,ij}|\nu_j|\nu_j\) with a full matrix.
  • A Froude–Krylov term is added for waves and gusts; the water itself does not rotate.
  • The image effect uses distances from the CG along world axes.
How it works

Thrusters and battery#

src/physics/thruster.js, src/physics/battery.js

PWM to thrust

A measured table (Blue Robotics T200 / T500 or your own) or a parametric curve gives static thrust from the PWM pulse. Inside the deadband thrust is exactly zero. A motor reversed with MOT_n_DIRECTION = −1 uses the curve mirrored about 1500 µs, so the strong side of the propeller stays the strong side.

\[ T_0(P)=\begin{cases} T_{fwd}\left(\dfrac{P-hi}{P_{max}-hi}\right)^{e} & P>hi\\[4pt] 0 & lo\le P\le hi\\[2pt] -T_{rev}\left(\dfrac{lo-P}{lo-P_{min}}\right)^{e} & P<lo\end{cases}\qquad lo,hi=1500\mp\delta,\qquad T_{reversed}(P)=T_0(3000-P) \]
defaults\(T_{fwd}=51.5\) N, \(T_{rev}=40.2\) N, \(\delta=25\) µs, \(e=1.25\), \(P\in[1100,1900]\)
thruster.js:12-21, 73-97, 164-170

Rotor speed, voltage and current

The propeller cannot change speed instantly. A normalised rotor speed \(n\) follows the command with a faster time constant when speeding up than when braking, and pauses at zero when it reverses (the ESC re-commutates). Thrust grows with \(n|n|\). Thrust and current follow the battery voltage through the Blue Robotics tables at 10–20 V.

\[ n_{cmd}=\operatorname{sgn}(T_{cmd})\sqrt{\frac{|T_{cmd}|}{T_{side}}},\qquad n_{k+1}=n_k+(n_{cmd}-n_k)\left(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau}\right),\qquad T=T_{side}\;n|n| \]
\[ T_{cmd}=T_0(P)\;s_V(V_{bus}),\qquad s_V=\frac{\mathcal I(T_{max},V)}{\mathcal I(T_{max},V_{nom})},\qquad I=I_0+I_{max}(V)\left[\frac{|T|}{T_{side}\,s_V}\right]^{1.5} \]
T200 @ 16 V\(\tau_{up}=0.05\) s, \(\tau_{down}=0.075\) s, reversal pause 0.03 s; full thrust 2.98–6.70 kgf forward and 13.6–32 A between 10 and 20 V; the ESC cuts out below 6.5 V
thruster.js:29-32, 141-197

In-flow, ventilation, hull and reaction torque

Thrust falls when water already flows through the propeller (advance ratio), near the surface where the propeller sucks air, and when the jet hits the hull. The spinning propeller also twists the vehicle; mirror-image thrusters get opposite hands so a correctly built vehicle cancels it.

\[ T_e=T\;\underbrace{\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(1-\frac{V_{in}}{V_0},0,1.25\right)}_{\text{in-flow}}\;\underbrace{\min\!\left(1,\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{1.5D}}\right)}_{\text{ventilation}}\;\underbrace{\left(1-t_d\right)}_{\text{jet on hull}},\qquad V_0=J_0\,D\,\frac{\text{rpm}_{max}}{60}\max(|n|,0.05) \]
\[ Q=-h\;\frac{K_Q}{K_T}\;D\;T,\qquad \frac{K_Q}{K_T}=0.12,\qquad \tau_{thr}=\sum_k\begin{bmatrix}T_{e,k}\,a_k\\ r_k\times T_{e,k}\,a_k+Q_k\,a_k\end{bmatrix} \]
\(t_d\)jet–hull deduction from 7 rays cast along the jet, up to 0.5 (forward and reverse separately)
\(J_0\)0.9 forward, 0.75 reverse for the T200
thruster.js:204-229, body.js:365-388, model.js:58-80

Battery

A Thevenin model: open-circuit voltage from state of charge, an internal resistance that rises when the pack is nearly empty, and one RC branch for the slow voltage recovery after a burst. Default: 4S Li-ion, 18 Ah, 0.03 Ω, plus 2 A for the electronics.

\[ V_t=n_{cells}\,\mathrm{OCV}(\mathrm{SoC})-I\,R(\mathrm{SoC})-V_1,\qquad V_{1,k+1}=V_{1,k}\,e^{-\Delta t/\tau_1}+I R_1\left(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau_1}\right) \]
\[ \mathrm{SoC}=1-\frac{\int I\,dt}{3.6\,C_{mAh}},\qquad R=R_0\left[1+0.6\,\frac{\max(0,0.3-s)}{0.3}+2.5\,\frac{\max(0,0.05-s)}{0.05}\right],\qquad R_1=\tfrac12R_0,\ \tau_1=30\ \text{s} \]
battery.js:11-81 · at full forward the DUBURI sample reads 15.9–16.0 V at 24–27 A
How it works

Added mass and damping from your design#

Where the matrices \(M_A\), \(D_L\) and \(D_Q\) come from. AUTO-TUNE runs both methods at several resolutions and reports an uncertainty for each value.

Mass properties

The hull is voxelised (cell ≈ largest size / 56, at least 3 mm). Displaced volume and CB are integrated exactly along rays in x, y and z, weighted by how well each axis sees the surface; thin 12.7 mm plates come out within 0.04 %. Inertia uses uniform density and the parallel-axis theorem about the CG, unless you enter CAD values.

\[ I_g=\rho_m I^{u}+m\left(|d|^2 I_3-d\,d^\top\right),\qquad \rho_m=\frac{m}{V_{vox}},\qquad M_G=H^\top M_O\,H,\quad H=\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-S(r)\\0&I_3\end{bmatrix} \]
model.js:41-56, bem.js:317-327 · H moves any 6×6 matrix from a reference point to the CG

Panel method (BEM) for added mass

A Hess–Smith constant-source panel method on a smooth mesh of the hull, for a deeply submerged body in ideal flow. Six problems, one per DOF, share one influence matrix and are solved with GMRES; then the solution is extrapolated from three mesh sizes (900, 1800, 3600 panels).

\[ \tfrac12\sigma_i+\sum_{k\ne i}K_{ik}\,\sigma_k=N_j(x_i),\qquad K_{ik}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S_k}\frac{n_i\cdot(x_i-\xi)}{|x_i-\xi|^3}\,dS,\qquad N=\begin{bmatrix}n\\ (r-r_{ref})\times n\end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \phi_{l,i}=\sum_k G_{ik}\,\sigma_{l,k},\quad G=-\frac{1}{4\pi|x-\xi|},\qquad m_{lj}=-\rho\sum_i \phi_{l,i}\,N_j(x_i)\,A_i,\qquad M_A=\tfrac12\left(m+m^\top\right) \]
bem.js:26-200 · GMRES restart 60, tolerance 10⁻⁸ · matches Capytaine within 0.2–2 % and the exact ellipsoid within 2 %

Before AUTO-TUNE runs, the default is a Lamb ellipsoid with the hull's proportions and volume, with strip-theory floors on the rotational terms, moved to the CG.

Lattice-Boltzmann CFD for damping

A virtual tow tank on the GPU. D3Q19 lattice, regularised BGK collision with a Smagorinsky turbulence model, Bouzidi interpolated walls (moving walls for rotation), and forces by momentum exchange.

\[ g_i^{eq}=w_i\left[(\rho-1)+\rho\left(3\,c_i\cdot u+4.5\,(c_i\cdot u)^2-1.5\,|u|^2\right)\right],\qquad g_i^{*}=g_i^{eq}+\left(1-\frac1\tau\right)4.5\,w_i\left(c_{i\alpha}c_{i\beta}-\tfrac13\delta_{\alpha\beta}\right)\Pi^{neq}_{\alpha\beta} \]
\[ \tau=\tfrac12\left(\tau_0+\sqrt{\tau_0^2+18\sqrt2\,C_s^2\,|\Pi^{neq}|/\rho}\right),\qquad \tau_0=3\nu_{lat}+\tfrac12,\qquad C_s=0.12 \]
\[ F=\sum_{\text{links}}\left[c_{\bar\imath}\left(g^{*}_{\bar\imath}+g_i+2w_i\right)-u_w\left(g^{*}_{\bar\imath}-g_i\right)\right] \]
cfd/d3q19.js, cpu.js:120-217, gpu.js · weights 1/3, 1/18, 1/36

Each DOF is towed (or spun) at two Reynolds numbers, 500 and 2000, on up to three grids. The coefficients are fitted to \(C(Re)=C_\infty+K/Re\) and scaled to your vehicle in water: \(C_\infty\) becomes quadratic damping, \(K\) linear damping. The fit is projected onto a dissipative matrix so it can never add energy.

\[ D_{Q,ij}=-\rho\,s_j^2\,L^{p_i}\,C_{\infty,i},\qquad D_{L,ij}=-\rho\,s_j\,L^{p_i-1}\,K_i\,\nu,\qquad p=2\ \text{(force)},\ 3\ \text{(moment)} \]
\[ \text{GCI}=\frac{1.25\,\left|\frac{f_1-f_2}{f_1}\right|}{r_{21}^{\,p}-1},\qquad f_{ext}=\frac{r_{21}^{\,p}f_1-f_2}{r_{21}^{\,p}-1}\qquad\text{(Celik et al. 2008)} \]
study.js:400-541, core/convergence.js:27-50 · sphere drag at Re 100: Richardson 1.074 vs 1.088 reference (−1.2 %)

Rotation runs use a frozen hull with a moving-wall condition, so rotational damping carries an extra 30 % model uncertainty.

How it works

ArduSub control#

Ported from ArduSub 4.5.7 and compared line by line with the C++ (libraries/AC_PID, AC_AttitudeControl, AP_Motors, ArduSub/). Units follow ArduPilot: centidegrees and cm at the interfaces.

The control chain for one tick, from stick to propeller:

  1. Pilot input (joystick.cpp): MANUAL_CONTROL x, y, z, r become RC channels 1–6, scaled by the pilot gain.
  2. Flight mode turns them into targets: lean angles, a yaw rate, a climb rate, or a position.
  3. Attitude controller: input shaping → quaternion error → angle P (square-root controller) → body-rate target.
  4. Rate PIDs: body-rate error → roll, pitch, yaw demands.
  5. Depth controller: position P → velocity PID → acceleration PID → throttle.
  6. Mixer (AP_Motors6DOF): six demands × factor table → one output per motor → PWM.

PID controllers

AC_PID is used for the rate loops (ATC_RAT_*) and vertical acceleration (PSC_ACCZ_*). Target and error are low-pass filtered, the derivative is taken on the filtered error, and the integrator stops growing while the motors are saturated.

\[ \bar r_k=\bar r_{k-1}+\alpha_T\,(r_k-\bar r_{k-1}),\qquad \bar e_k=\bar e_{k-1}+\alpha_E\left(\bar r_{k-1}-y_k-\bar e_{k-1}\right),\qquad \bar d_k=\bar d_{k-1}+\alpha_D\left(\frac{\bar e_k-\bar e_{k-1}}{\Delta t}-\bar d_{k-1}\right) \]
\[ \alpha(\Delta t,f_c)=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t+\frac{1}{2\pi f_c}},\qquad I_k=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(I_{k-1}+K_i\,\bar e_k\,\Delta t,\ \pm I_{max}\right)\ \text{unless saturated} \]
\[ u_k=\mu\left(K_p\,\bar e_k+K_d\,\bar d_k\right)+I_k \]
\(f_c\)FLTT, FLTE, FLTD cut-offs (Hz); 0 disables the filter
\(\mu\)slew-limiter gain reduction from SMAX (1 when SMAX = 0); PDMX caps \(|P+D|\)
defaultsroll/pitch P 0.135, I 0.09, D 0.0036, IMAX 0.444; yaw P 0.18, I 0.018, IMAX 0.222
pid.js:75-136 · AC_PID.cpp:193-320 · in ArduSub 4.5.7 the rate loop does not add FF, so ATC_RAT_*_FF and D_FF have no effect

The SMAX slew limiter watches how fast P+D changes. When the slew rate stays above SMAX it lowers the gain, which stops oscillation before it builds up:

\[ \mu=\begin{cases}\dfrac{S}{S+1.5\,(\rho_{mod}-S)} & \rho_{mod}>S\\[4pt] 1 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\qquad \rho=\text{peak slew rate of }(P+D)\text{ over 300 ms windows, filtered at 25 Hz} \]
control.js:343-387 · SlewLimiter.cpp:29-135

The square-root controller is ArduPilot's shaped P controller: linear near the target, then a square root so the commanded rate never asks for more than the allowed acceleration.

\[ \dot x(e)=\begin{cases} K_p\,e & |e|\le \dfrac{a_{max}}{K_p^2}\\[6pt] \operatorname{sgn}(e)\sqrt{2a_{max}\left(|e|-\dfrac{a_{max}}{2K_p^2}\right)} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\qquad \dot x\leftarrow\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\dot x,\ \pm\frac{|e|}{\Delta t}\right) \]
control.js:23-38 · AP_Math/control.cpp:401-433

Attitude controller

The target attitude is a quaternion \(q_T\). Pilot inputs are shaped into a smooth feed-forward rate \(\omega_t\) with time constant ATC_INPUT_TC, and \(q_T\) advances by \(\omega_t\Delta t\) each tick. The error to the measured attitude \(q_B\) is split into a tilt part and a heading part, and each goes through the angle P.

\[ \omega_d\leftarrow\omega_d+\operatorname{sqrt\_ctrl}\!\left(\theta_e,\ \tfrac{1}{\tau_{in}},\ \alpha_{max},\ \Delta t\right),\qquad \omega_t^{+}=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\omega_d,\ \omega_t\pm\alpha_{max}\Delta t\right) \]
\[ \omega_{c,i}=\operatorname{sqrt\_ctrl}\!\left(e_i,\ K_{ang,i},\ \tfrac12\alpha_i,\ \Delta t\right),\qquad \omega_b=\omega_c+\left(q_B^{-1}q_T\right)\omega_t,\qquad u_{roll}=\mathrm{PID}_R(\omega_{b,x},\hat\omega_x) \]
\[ \omega=\begin{bmatrix}\dot\phi-\sin\theta\,\dot\psi\\ \cos\phi\,\dot\theta+\sin\phi\cos\theta\,\dot\psi\\ -\sin\phi\,\dot\theta+\cos\theta\cos\phi\,\dot\psi\end{bmatrix}\qquad\text{(Euler rates to body rates)} \]
defaultsATC_ANG_*_P 6, ATC_ACCEL_*_MAX 110 000 cd/s² (1100 °/s²), ATC_INPUT_TC 0.15 s, ATC_RATE_Y_MAX 180 °/s
blendabove 30° tilt error the feed-forward fades out, above 60° only the tilt is corrected

Depth and position

AC_PosControl runs a cascade: position error → velocity target → acceleration target → throttle around the hover value. Pilot climb commands are jerk-limited by PSC_JERK_Z and the filter cut-offs.

\[ v_T=\operatorname{sqrt\_ctrl}\!\left(z_T-\hat z,\ K_{p,z}\right)+v_d,\qquad a_T=\mathrm{PID}_{vz}(v_T,\hat v_z)+a_d,\qquad u_{thr}=\frac{\mathrm{PID}_{az}(a_T,\,a_{z,meas})}{1000}+h \]
\[ \dot z_{pilot}=\begin{cases}V_{dn}\dfrac{c-(500-\delta)}{500-\delta} & c<500-\delta\\[6pt] V_{up}\dfrac{c-(500+\delta)}{500-\delta} & c>500+\delta\\[4pt] 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases},\qquad \delta=\texttt{THR\_DZ}\cdot G \]
\(h\)hover throttle MOT_THST_HOVER, forced to 0.5 at boot by ArduSub

poscontrol.js:131-265 · AC_PosControl.cpp:701-1010, ArduSub/Attitude.cpp:90-125

The XY controller (Position Hold, Guided, Auto) works the same way in two dimensions. ArduSub turns its lean-angle output into forward and lateral thrust instead of tilting the vehicle:

\[ \theta_T=\arctan\frac{-a_f}{100g},\qquad \phi_T=\arctan\frac{a_r\cos\theta_T}{100g},\qquad F=\frac{\operatorname{clamp}(-\theta_T,\pm A)}{A},\quad L=\frac{\operatorname{clamp}(\phi_T,\pm A)}{A} \]

Motor mixer

Each motor has a row of six factors (roll, pitch, yaw, throttle, forward, lateral) from the FRAME_CONFIG table or from the automatic allocation. MOT_n_DIRECTION multiplies the result. For the Vectored 6DOF frame (the DUBURI file, FRAME_CONFIG = 2) the attitude-plus-throttle part and the yaw-plus-translation part are normalised separately:

\[ \mathrm{rpt}_i=\rho\,r_i+\pi\,p_i+T\,t_i,\qquad \mathrm{yfl}_i=\gamma\,y_i+F\,f_i+L\,l_i,\qquad u_i=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(d_i\left(\frac{\mathrm{rpt}_i}{m_1}+\frac{\mathrm{yfl}_i}{m_2}\right),\pm1\right) \]
\[ m_1=\max\!\left(1,\max_i|\mathrm{rpt}_i|\right),\quad m_2=\max\!\left(1,\max_i|\mathrm{yfl}_i|\right),\qquad \mathrm{PWM}_i=1500+u_i\cdot 400\ \ \text{(MOT\_PWM 1100–1900)} \]
motors.js:306-436 · AP_Motors6DOF.cpp:233-559 · other frames use one clamp per motor; Vectored (FRAME_CONFIG 1) limits forward thrust by MOT_FV_CPLNG_K

A spool state machine (shut down → ground idle → spooling up → unlimited) ramps the output over MOT_SPOOL_TIME (0.5 s) after arming. MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX scales every output down when the predicted current is too high. Motor \(m\) drives the servo output whose SERVOn_FUNCTION is 33 + \(m\), and the ESC sees the previous tick’s value, as on the Pixhawk.

  3. Motors output.

AUTO-TUNE#

src/sim/tuning.js. Every test runs on a fresh copy of your vehicle in open water; results are suggestions and nothing is applied until you click.

  1. Hydrodynamics. Converged panel-method added mass and CFD damping for your hull, each with its uncertainty.
  2. Rate loops, inner loop first, following ArduPilot's AC_AutoTune: twitch the axis, raise D until the rate bounces back more than 10 % (AGGR 0.1), then raise P until the rate overshoots, then bisect between good and bad.
  3. Angle P by angle twitches of 20° (30° in yaw), backed off to 90 %; ATC_ACCEL_*_MAX from the measured acceleration.
  4. ATC_INPUT_TC: kept unless the tuned loops overshoot more than 10 % with it.
  5. Depth (accel → velocity → position) and, with a DVL, position gains by a grid search refined with golden-section steps on the step-response cost.
  6. Check: each axis is compared with your current gains on the full tuned set; an axis that was better before keeps its gains.
\[ J=0.5\,T_s+0.05\,\mathrm{OS}+0.1\max(0,\mathrm{OS}-10)+0.4\,N_{osc}+3\,\mathrm{sat}+0.6\,T_r+2\,\frac{\mathrm{RMS}_{tail}}{\text{band}},\qquad J_{eval}=0.6\,J(\text{step})+0.4\,J\!\left(-\tfrac12\text{step}\right) \]
\(T_r,\ T_s\)10–90 % rise time and settling time (s); OS overshoot (%); \(N_{osc}\) oscillations; sat fraction of time the mixer saturated
marginrate P and D are multiplied by 0.85, and every value is clamped to the ArduSub parameter range

Accuracy and limits#

Engineering estimates of how closely the real vehicle will behave like the simulation, assuming you uploaded your own design with measured mass and CG. They have not been checked against pool data yet.

WhatEstimated match0 – 100 %Why

In short: the kind of behaviour (which way it moves, whether a gain set oscillates, what each mode does) matches about 90 %; the numbers (speed, overshoot, settling time) about 70–80 %. Gains tuned here are in the right range but may need 10–30 % adjustment in the pool.

Not modelled

  • The tether. A real tether adds drag and pulls the vehicle, most in sway and at depth.
  • Wake interaction between thrusters, windmilling drag of a stopped propeller, wave radiation damping.
  • EKF3 itself (a simpler estimator with realistic noise stands in), GPS, and a real MAVLink connection.
  • Temperature changing with depth; thrusters and electronics changing with pressure.
Make it trustworthy

Weigh the vehicle, find the CG with a hang test, and, if you can, measure thrust on a stand. Then do three simple pool runs (full forward, full up, a yaw turn) and compare the logs with the same runs here.

Presenting the simulator#

A one-minute pitch, a five-minute demo and the questions a supervisor is likely to ask.

One-minute pitch

"Sir, the DUBURI simulator lets us test the robot on a computer before it goes in the water. It uses our own 3D design and the exact parameter file that is on our Pixhawk."

"Inside, the ArduSub 4.5.7 control code is ported line by line and checked against the ArduPilot source. The motion is computed with the Fossen 6-DOF equations that marine robotics uses."

"So we can check the thruster layout, the PID gains and depth hold before a pool session. That saves pool time and battery, and lowers the risk of damaging the vehicle."

"The numbers won't match exactly. Our estimate is about 90 % for the kind of behaviour and 70–80 % for numbers like speed and overshoot. One measured pool test would let us calibrate it."

Five-minute demo

  1. Open the simulator and click Fly the sample first.
  2. Press Enter to arm, fly with W A S D and R/F.
  3. Press 3 (Depth Hold), let go of the keys, press G: the vehicle keeps its depth and the graphs show target and actual.
  4. Open Frame and move the Surge slider: show which thrusters fire and the force that comes out.
  5. Open Params: our 943 Pixhawk parameters are loaded; change a gain and the effect is immediate.
  6. Open Tuning and run a yaw step test: rise time, overshoot and settling time appear.
  7. Set FS_GCS_ENABLE to 3, click GCS link: about 2.5 s later the vehicle switches to Depth Hold on its own.
  8. Upload our design (OBJ) and show the thrusters being found automatically.

AUTO-TUNE runs the panel method and CFD and can take several minutes; start it before the meeting if you want to show its result.

Likely questions

Is this the real ArduSub firmware?

No. It is a JavaScript port of the ArduSub 4.5.7 control code (motors, attitude, PID, depth and position control, modes, failsafes, joystick), compared line by line with the C++ source. It is not the firmware binary (that would be SITL), and EKF3 is replaced by a simpler estimator.

How close is it to the real robot?

About 90 % for the kind of behaviour and 70–80 % for the numbers, as an estimate. The largest uncertainties are drag (CFD, ±20–30 %), the missing tether and the mass and CG we enter. See Accuracy and limits.

Why not ArduPilot SITL with Gazebo?

SITL runs the real firmware and is a good cross-check, but it needs a Linux setup and a separately built physics model. DUBURI runs in any browser, builds the hydrodynamic model from our own CAD, and loads our Pixhawk file directly. Both can be used together.

What do we need to measure?

Mass in air, centre of gravity, ideally thrust per thruster, and one short pool test to compare with the simulation.

Can the exported parameters harm the vehicle?

The export is an ordinary .param file with your original values plus the ones you changed. Review the changed values in QGroundControl before writing them, as with any parameter change.

Troubleshooting#

"Compass performance degraded" every time I arm

The DUBURI file has GPS_TYPE = 1 and there is no EKF origin, so ArduSub prints this warning. Real ArduSub does the same. Set GPS_TYPE = 0 if the vehicle has no GPS.

"Mode change failed: … requires position"

Position Hold, Surface, Guided, Auto and Circle need a position estimate. Tick Positioning system present (DVL / USBL) in Pool & water, only if the real vehicle has one.

"Mode change failed: … need alt estimate"

Depth modes need a working depth sensor. Check the depth sensor in Pool & water and BARO_EXT_BUS / BARO_PROBE_EXT.

The vehicle will not arm

Read the PreArm banner on the HUD: it gives ArduSub's own reason. Arming also needs a joystick button mapped to arm or arm-toggle, and is refused while the GCS link is down.

The gamepad is not detected

Press any button once. Use Chrome or Edge. Some embedded views hide controllers: save the page from Logs & files and open the file directly.

The 3D view is blank

The simulator needs WebGL2. Turn on hardware acceleration in the browser settings, or try Chrome or Edge.

A thruster was detected that is not a thruster

Short hollow parts 85–115 mm across look like a T200 duct. Discard the detection or rename the part so it contains "mount" or "housing".
